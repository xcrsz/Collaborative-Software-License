# Collaborative Software License (CSL)

The Collaborative Software License (CSL) is an independent software-license project focused on clear grants of rights, voluntary cooperation, and predictable limits on termination.

CSL is designed to permit commercial and noncommercial use, modification, combination with other material, and redistribution, including proprietary derivative works, subject to the license's express terms.

## Design goals

CSL is being developed around the following goals:

- **Clear grants.** State the copyright and patent permissions a Licensor grants, and limit those grants to rights the Licensor owns or is authorized to license.
- **Broad permitted use.** Permit lawful commercial and noncommercial use, modification, combination, and redistribution, including proprietary derivative works.
- **Defined patent scope.** Limit patent coverage to claims within the license's stated scope, including qualifying claims acquired or controlled by a Licensor later.
- **Individual responsibility.** Limit termination to the relevant rights granted to the party whose rights are being terminated, without automatically revoking independent grants to compliant downstream recipients.
- **Voluntary participation.** Do not require membership, donations, advocacy, endorsement, or project participation as a condition of exercising the licensed rights.
- **No mandatory registration.** Do not require click-through acceptance or registration, and do not treat mere receipt or silence as contractual assent.
- **Textual independence.** Do not make the license dependent on approval, classification, or ongoing authority from an external organization.
- **Explicit legal limits.** Recognize that mandatory law may affect the interpretation or enforceability of particular provisions.

CSL draws philosophical inspiration from individual rights and voluntary cooperation. It does not require users, contributors, or recipients to adopt any philosophy or affiliation.

## Main provisions

The current working text contains 15 sections covering:

1. Definitions
2. Copyright license
3. Patent license and patent-litigation termination
4. Permitted uses
5. Redistribution and downstream grants
6. Trademarks
7. Disclaimer of warranty
8. Limitation of liability
9. Termination and cure
10. Individual freedom and voluntary use
11. Fixed text, version identity, and independence
12. Applicable law and severability
13. No obligation to provide services
14. Scope of grant and separate agreements
15. Exercise of rights and contractual assent
